# Who uses the AI tool, and the No-AI option, 2026-10-05

`content/ai-use.json` is the sixth data stage (`tools/ai_use.py`). It runs after the two
labeled sets and pins its input and output like the earlier stages. It makes three changes:

| Change | Result |
|---|---|
| A reviewed `op` field on every activity: who operates an AI tool | 1,078 activities, each with one of five labeled values |
| The No-AI limit renamed to what it now means | “My students won’t use an AI tool themselves” admits **604** activities (204 before) |
| Activities in which AI is neither used nor discussed withdrawn | **4** withdrawn; 3 sources left with no activity removed. Both tools serve **1,078 activities / 637 sources** |

Nothing else in any record changes. `tests/ai_use_test.py` checks that every kept record
differs from its input only by `op`.

## 1. The `op` field

`op` records who operates an AI tool in the activity as its summary describes it. It is
not the same as `ac`, which records who does the activity. In a student activity the
instructor may be the only one who operates a tool, and in an instructor's assignment
design students may operate one.

| Value | Label | Description shown to faculty | Licensed | Sets | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `students` | Students | Students operate an AI tool themselves. | 342 | 181 | 523 |
| `faculty_or_staff` | Faculty or staff only | Only the instructor, or the faculty or staff member doing the work, operates an AI tool. Students, if involved, work with what it produced. | 311 | 90 | 401 |
| `optional` | Optional | The activity is complete without an AI tool. An AI step is offered as an option. | 37 | 0 | 37 |
| `none` | No one | No one operates an AI tool. AI is what the activity examines. | 76 | 1 | 77 |
| `not_specified` | Not stated | An AI tool is used, but the record does not say whether students operate it. | 9 | 31 | 40 |

The vocabulary is published as `operators` in `acts.json`. Activity details show the
description under *At a glance* as **Who uses an AI tool**. The Register adds a **Who uses
an AI tool** filter (`#activities?op=none`) under *How the AI and people divide the work*.

### How the values were assigned

Each value was read from the published summary. The change note and the library's
suggested adaptations were treated as context, so a tool named only there does not count
as used. Reviewers worked to written definitions in three passes:

1. **All 779 licensed activities** were classified for `op`, and for whether AI is used,
   is the subject, appears only in an adaptation, or does not appear.
2. **A second review of every value that would let the limit admit an activity**
   (`none`, or `faculty_or_staff` on a student activity). This covered 105 records; another
   52 had already been settled by hand from the full record. The second reviewer disagreed
   on 24. Seventeen changes were accepted, and seven were settled differently after reading
   the whole record.
3. **A student-use check of the 293 instructor- and staff-led licensed activities**, and a
   classification of **all 303 set records** from scratch. Of the licensed activities, 22
   were flagged. Sixteen changes were accepted, and six were settled differently.

Where reviewers disagreed, two rules settled the reading:

- **The main route decides.** If the instructor operates the tool and students prompting it
  is offered as a variant, the value is `faculty_or_staff`. The activity can be run without
  students operating anything. Examples: CAN-B-CASE-11, CAN-B-ASMT-05, CAN-B-VERI-11, and
  CAN-W-oer-029.
- **Participants who are faculty or staff are not students.** In a workshop for educators or
  researchers, the participants' own tool use is `faculty_or_staff` (CAN-W-gov-005,
  CAN-W-workflow-006). Certificate learners and teacher trainees count as students.

When a summary does not establish whether students operate the tool, the value is
`not_specified`. The limit treats it as unknown, never as a confirmed fit. Every value
other than `students` carries, in the manifest, the quotation from the summary that
settles it.

## 2. The No-AI option

| | Label | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Before | I’d rather my students not use AI at all | Shows only activities where AI is deliberately absent, or that carry a stated non-AI route. |
| After | My students won’t use an AI tool themselves | Includes activities where only the instructor uses an AI tool, activities that examine AI without one, and activities with a stated route that uses no AI. |

The rule in `src/js/matching.js` is as follows.

- A stated route without AI (`na`) always qualifies.
- Otherwise, `faculty_or_staff`, `optional`, and `none` are confirmed.
- `students` is excluded.
- `not_specified`, or a record without `op`, is unknown. These activities appear only under
  *Requirements not yet confirmed*.

The Finder's note under the limit and the label used for an unconfirmed requirement both
say the same thing. In The Register, the same rule is behind the **Students use no AI tool**
requirement, and the no-AI type card was renamed to match:

| | Name | Text |
|---|---|---|
| Before | Activities that use no AI tool | Some activities are designed so that no AI tool is used at all, for example, examining AI-generated examples supplied in advance, or discussing AI without using it. Others describe a route that works without AI. What If AI lets you ask for these directly. |
| After | Activities where students use no AI tool | In some activities students never operate an AI tool themselves: the instructor prepares AI output in advance and students work with it, or the class examines AI without using any tool. Others describe a route that works without AI. What If AI and the activity filters let you ask for these directly. |

Its count is the number the limit confirms.

### What changed in results

- **604 activities are now confirmed, up from 204.** Of the 403 newly admitted:
  - 307 are instructor- or staff-led;
  - 58 are student activities in which only the instructor operates a tool;
  - 23 offer AI only as an option;
  - 15 examine AI without a tool.
- **27 are now listed as requirements to verify** because the record does not say who
  operates the tool. None of them was admitted before except CAN-B-ETHI-04.
- **Two activities the old rule admitted are now excluded:** CAN-A2-A-074 and CAN-A2-A-081.
  Students operate an AI tool in both, but each is recorded with capability `none_required`
  (see section 5).

## 3. Withdrawn: AI neither used nor discussed

In each of these activities, as published, no AI tool is used and AI is not discussed. AI
appeared only in a compilers' suggestion that the published record does not carry, or not
at all. The stage re-checks the rule, refusing a withdrawal whose title, summary, gate, or
output names AI, a model, or an algorithm. It also refuses one that a remix builds on.

| Activity | What it is | Source left without an activity |
|---|---|---|
| CAN-L-045 | Collating two editions of a literary text with comparison software | CSR-0476 (removed) |
| CAN-L-055 | FAIR-data workshop: metadata and repository choice | CSR-0486 (removed with CAN-L-056) |
| CAN-L-056 | FAIR-data self-assessment and action plan | CSR-0486 |
| CAN-L-075 | One-day reproducibility hackathon on published papers | CSR-0505 (removed) |

The change notes for CAN-L-055 and CAN-L-056 say they were included "as a non-AI route".
The imported release keeps all four records.

## 4. Decisions left open

These activities were kept. Each is a judgment about the edge of the collection, and none
is withdrawn without that decision.

**Update, 2026-10-06:** item 3 is decided. The 26 activities, and eight more found in the audit,
are withdrawn by the record review, and the remixes built on them are held; see
[the record review](RECORD-REVIEW-2026-10-06.md). Items 1, 2, 4 and 5 remain open and are flagged
there.

1. **Remix parents with no AI in the activity.** CAN-L-043 (a grant-style digital humanities
   proposal) and CAN-L-073 (rerunning a deposited dataset and critiquing its README) neither
   use nor discuss AI. CAN-L-042 (corpus frequencies with a counting tool) mentions a
   chatbot only as a contrast. They are the parents of WIA-R-CODE-04, WIA-R-CODE-02, and
   WIA-R-MMOD-01, each of which adds an AI operation. Withdrawing a parent would leave its
   remix without the published activity it builds on, and the stage refuses it until that
   is decided.
2. **Procedural and rule-based text generators.** CAN-B-PROC-01, PROC-04, PROC-05, PROC-06,
   PROC-09, and PROC-10 belong to the *Computational Poetics and Procedural Generation*
   family. Their tools are template grammars, Tracery, Curveship, RiTa Markov chains, and a
   p5.js loop, and none involves AI or machine learning. PROC-09 mentions a large language
   model only as a contrast. PROC-05 and PROC-10 are remix parents (WIA-R-CONV-07,
   WIA-R-AUDI-10). Their `op` is `none`. Whether procedural generation counts as being about
   AI is the decision.
3. **An optional AI step added to a non-AI source.** In 26 activities the source is a work on
   feedback, assessment, or instructional design that does not involve AI. The change note
   reads "AI role and deliverable specified editorially", and AI appears only in a closing
   "AI can…" sentence with a sample prompt:
   - CAN-A2-A-038, 039, 040, 041, 043, 044, 045, 046, 047
   - CAN-A2-A-049, 059, 067, 069, 071, 099, 132, 141
   - CAN-A2-A-209, 210, 211, 212, 213, 214, 216, 217, 228

   They carry `op: optional`. CAN-A2-A-067, 069, and 099 are remix parents. The other 11
   `optional` records are about AI or come from sources that involve it, and are not in
   question.
4. **AI only as framing or rationale.**
   - CAN-L-040: the eGFR race coefficient. The source concerns a clinical formula, and the
     library's summary makes the bridge to AI in health care.
   - CAN-W-stem-017: hand-worked statistics problems, justified by AI-written code.
   - CAN-A1-EQ-060: the summary says "tool" throughout. The source is about AI writing tools.
   - CAN-W-lis-018: a citation log designed against misuse of generative AI. The summary
     never names AI.
   - CAN-B-PROF-11: product ideas from a random generator, with students writing "without AI".
5. **Adjacent technologies, kept in scope.** Social-bot detection (CAN-W-gapdisc-018),
   citation-network tools (CAN-R3-006), recommender algorithms (CAN-L-004, CAN-L-067), and
   non-generative machine learning. No change is proposed.

## 5. Data issues noted, not changed

- CAN-A2-A-074 and CAN-A2-A-081 record capability `none_required`, although students
  operate an AI tool in both. The activity details no longer say "This activity runs without
  any AI tool" for them, because that sentence now follows `op`. *AI tool needed* still
  shows the recorded capability.
- CAN-B-PROC-01, PROC-04, PROC-05, PROC-06, and PROC-10 record `text_chat`, which places
  rule-based generators under *Conversational AI* in The Register.

Both issues are capability coding for the pipeline to settle.

## 6. Contract

`op` on every activity and the top-level `operators` exist only in the site's staged output.
The export contract stays at 1, and `check_release.py` is unchanged. When the pipeline
exports `op` itself, it should move into the export together with `tier`, `par`, `dep`, and
`chk` (see [the sets record](TIERS-2026-10-05.md), section 6).
