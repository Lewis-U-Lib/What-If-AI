# Result order before and after, October 15, 2026

What If AI now orders each group of results by fit first: the match score, then how near any miss is,
then how targeted the use-case idea is to what was asked, then origin (an adaptation of a published
activity before one written for the collection), then reported use, then a fixed neutral order. The
import-era quality tier no longer counts. The rules are in `docs/MATCHING.md`.

## Across every combination of answers

All 43,740 combinations of work, task, field, scale, level, and setting were run against the released
collection with no limits set. “First nine” is what a reader sees before choosing “Show more”.

| Group | Lists | First nine changed | Written for the collection, share of first nine | Tier-0 adaptations, share of first nine |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exact matches | 2,086 | 3% | 57% → 59% | 1% → 1% |
| Possible fits | 7,897 | 17% | 7% → 7% | 34% → 37% |
| Close matches | 35,266 | 41% | 19% → 21% | 12% → 18% |
| Broader starting points | 43,740 | 71% | 24% → 29% | 1% → 3% |

In close matches, the average distance of the one miss among the first nine fell from 4.68 to 4.55
(on the 0–6 scale), and the tier no longer pushes the one batch of adaptations that had tier 0 down
the list. Ideas that are equal on every fit measure, origin, and reported use still fall back to the
fixed neutral order; that remains the largest single deciding step, because many ideas share the
same tags.
