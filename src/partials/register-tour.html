The reference companion

The Register is the reference companion to What If AI. What If AI asks a few questions and suggests use-case ideas; The Register lets you browse and search everything in the collection directly.

How to use What If AI: a short walkthrough

What is here

Use-case ideas: teaching, research, and administrative use-case ideas that use generative AI or deliberately leave it out, each with its source and license.

Types of AI systems: what different kinds of AI tools do, described by capability, with a few current products named as examples.

Course AI policies: a spectrum of positions with openly licensed examples from published syllabi.

Sources: the published works the use-case ideas draw on.

Reading a use-case idea: who, how big, which tools

Who does it
Whether students carry out the use-case idea, or you do it as part of your own teaching, research, or service work.
Scale
The size of the piece of work, from a single class to a term-long project or a recurring process.
AI use
The kind of tool needed, described by capability rather than product. See Types of AI systems.

Reading a use-case idea: judgment and cautions

How the work is divided
What the AI tool does, and what people do with its output: the judgment that stays with people.
Course AI policy
The course policy position the use-case idea assumes. See Course AI policies.
Before you use it
Cautions about data, disclosure, cost, access, and accessibility.

Source and license

Tool license: {{license:label}} (opens in a new tab). Each use-case idea and source keeps its own license, shown with the item.

Each use-case idea shows where it comes from, its license, the attribution to keep, and how this version differs from the source.

Use-case ideas adapt only works published under a Creative Commons license or another open license. Some synthesis use-case ideas also link to a published source item that may not be adapted (it has no clear open license, allows no changes, or needs access first), and say so. That item, like any work cited only for its ideas, keeps its own terms. The library keeps a full record of sources, licenses, and editorial decisions for the collection.

How the collection was assembled

The use-case ideas come from openly licensed teaching and research literature, open educational resources, and published prompt and workflow libraries. Two smaller sets, the synthesis set and the remix set, were written for the collection; they are labeled on every use-case idea and have not yet been taught or tried.

Each is described and classified with a shared set of descriptors, so the collection can be searched by the kind of work rather than by the product name. Descriptions of what a source reports are kept separate from the library’s editorial additions, and suggested adaptations are labeled as suggestions.

Saving and printing

Use Save on any use-case idea, here or in What If AI, to collect it. Open Saved use-case ideas from the top of the page to review your selection, remove items, or print the set or save it as a PDF.

Your saved selection stays in this browser and is not sent to the library.

Use-case idea feedback

Open a use-case idea and choose Give feedback to share whether you are exploring it, considering it, planning to use it, have used it, or find it unsuitable for your needs. A short follow-up lets you say how it went or why it was not a fit. Every response is optional, and no name or email is collected.

Choose Send feedback when you are ready. This browser remembers your submitted answer, and you can update it later.

Corrections and questions

If a link has broken, a license or attribution looks wrong, or you have used a use-case idea and want to share how it went, please send feedback (opens in a new tab).

For help finding sources or planning a use-case idea, use Ask Us from the ✦ help button.
