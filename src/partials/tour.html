How to use What If AI

What the tool does

What If AI suggests openly licensed activities for your teaching, research or administrative work. You answer up to five short questions about what you are working on, and it matches activities from the collection to your answers.

Some activities use generative AI and some deliberately leave it out. Every one lists its source and license.

Describe your work

The questions ask about your work, not about AI products: what you are working on, what you would like to do, your field, how large a piece of work you have in mind, and anything that is off the table, such as using no AI tool or keeping student work out of AI tools.

You can skip any question. Use Back, the browser’s back button, or your list of answers to change an answer at any time.

Review the activities that fit

Results separate exact matches, possible fits with unspecified details, and close matches that name a preference to adjust. Activities with unconfirmed requirements appear in a separate group. Each card keeps its summary, field, scale, AI use, and available cost information.

Choose View details to read the whole activity.

Look inside an activity

The details open with the activity itself and a summary at a glance. They then explain how people and the AI tool divide the work, and what to consider before using it: data, the course AI policy it assumes, disclosure and risks.

They also give a route without AI, notes on adapting it, what the source reports, and the source, license and attribution to keep when you reuse it.

Save the ones you want

Select Save on a card or in an activity’s details. Open Saved activities from the top bar to review or remove your selection. It is the same list here and in The Register.

Your selection is kept in this browser only. There is no account, and nothing is sent anywhere.

Print or save your collection

In Saved activities, choose Print or save as PDF. The printout contains only your saved activities, one to a page, each with its full description, citation, license and attribution.

Go deeper in The Register

Open in The Register, at the foot of an activity’s details, opens the same activity in The Register, the reference companion to What If AI.

There you can search and filter the whole collection, read about types of AI systems and course AI policy positions, and browse every source the activities draw on.
